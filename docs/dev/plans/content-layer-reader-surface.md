# Content-layer reader surface (one host, two providers)

> **Standing rules for every session working this surface.** The program rules bind here and are not restated: they live in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which loads every session (write-once, typed capability slots over nullable soup, derive-or-do-not-own, the pin-once ladder, decline expiry). For the program context read [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md) and [unified-content-ui.md](unified-content-ui.md) first. Three more bind on this surface specifically. **The image viewer classes are never diverged further**: `PagerViewer` and `WebtoonViewer` carry five pre-existing `// RK` markers between them (the `seedColor` island in each, plus `WebtoonViewer`'s `volumeScrollDistance` and the two `handleKeyEvent` branches that use it) and `WebGpuViewer` carries none, so the rule is that no step adds a sixth. A step that wants to restructure one has stopped being this takeover and become the reverted Option A below. **Position is a typed capability, never an `Int`**, with one named exception: manga reads a page index, novels read a fraction, and the sole surviving integer is `ReaderChapter.requestedPage`, which the three image viewers read directly and which therefore stays exactly as it is (see "The restore path keeps its integer" below). And **the engine owns the viewer contract, not Mihon's `Viewer`**: `Viewer` becomes one adapter under a neutral contract rather than the interface a novel viewer implements, because it cannot carry a novel chapter without the mechanism this plan rejects (ruled, see Shape C below). The reader is the only surface in the program whose host is an Activity, so the usual "shared engine above two live models" shape bends here in one specific way, described under the amendment.

## Goal

One Reikai-owned reader orchestration serving manga and light novels from a single host, with the manga and novel engines staying separate underneath it. Novels move off the bespoke WebView plus vendored LNReader `core.js` reader onto **two rendering modes ported from tsundoku, a native text viewer and a first-party WebView viewer**, entering the same host, the same chrome, the same chapter navigator and the same settings as manga.

**Both rendering modes ship, and the vendored JS goes either way** (owner, 2026-09-03). The native TextView mode is the default; the WebView mode exists because a text layout cannot render a source's own CSS faithfully, which is a capability some sources need, so it is a typed capability slot rather than redundancy. What is being replaced is not "a WebView" but an unmaintainable one: our reader runs 793 lines of verbatim-vendored LNReader `core.js` plus two minified third-party bundles (`van.js`, `text-vibe.js`) and a 91-line polyfill, none of which we can update. Tsundoku's WebView mode runs 329 lines of their own readable JS across four files (`scroll-tracking.js` 258, `next-chapter-button.js` 30, `reader-chrome.js` 26, `inject-styles.js` 15) under about 1160 lines of Kotlin stylers and document builders. So the port replaces a dead-end engine with a maintained one in both modes, and `assets/lnreader-web/` is deleted whichever mode the user picks.

**Seamless chapter-to-chapter reading is a delivered capability, not a follow-up.** Scrolling out of one chapter straight into the next, the way the manga webtoon viewer already does, was one of the original reasons for the migration and it ships with it.

This is the sixth surface of the content-layer program ([content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md)) and its fourth orchestration takeover, after the library, the migrate flow and the recents feeds. It runs next, ahead of the downloads unification (Road B), which closes the program (owner, 2026-09-03). It supersedes [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md), whose chrome-only scope was ruled before the program's seam was settled, and absorbs the engine choice from [novel-reader-tsundoku.md](novel-reader-tsundoku.md), whose "fold novels into `ReaderActivity` the way tsundoku does" framing does not survive this design.

## Why

**The behaviour is forked, and the fork is total.** There is no shared reader orchestration at all today. Manga runs `ReaderActivity` plus `ReaderViewModel`; novels run a Voyager screen plus `NovelReaderScreenModel`, the last model in the app still on `ScreenModel` and still resolving eighteen types through Injekt. Every reader behaviour exists twice: chapter navigation, progress, mark-as-read, history, tracking, download-ahead, delete-after-read, orientation, keep-screen-on, brightness, and the settings sheet. The shipped chrome sharing reaches two composables (`ReaderContentOverlay` and `ReaderTopBar`); `ReaderAppBars`, `ReaderBottomBar`, `ChapterNavigator` and `ReaderPageIndicator` remain manga-only.

**The sync cost is the highest of any surface taken over so far, and the divergence is the lowest.** Twelve months of upstream history at the synced base: `ReaderActivity.kt` took 20 commits (+321/-344), `ReaderViewModel.kt` 15 (+201/-169), the whole `ui/reader/` tree 42, the viewer package another 20, and `presentation/reader/` 16. Against that, Reikai's `ReaderActivity` is 1020 lines to upstream's 944 with 16 `// RK` markers, and `ReaderViewModel` 1339 to 1022 with 54 (re-counted 2026-09-04; the earlier 46 was low). **This inverts the library takeover's justification and the amendment does not borrow it.** The library was ruled partly because its sync tax was already sunk (1076 lines to upstream's 837, 76 markers, 851 diverging lines), so every sync was already a hand-merge. The reader host still merges close to mechanically. The case here rests entirely on the duplicate-implementation cost of two reader hosts, which is the failure mode the 2026-08-06 ruling exists to remove, and on the fact that the novel half is being rebuilt regardless: its engine is a vendored JS file nobody can update.

**The user-facing prize is real and already asked for.** `unseensnick/Reikai#55` asks for a reader settings surface that does not require hunting, and the app-level Settings > Reader screen is 68 rows with novel content scattered across three non-adjacent groups distinguished only by a title suffix. Novels also gain the whole manga chrome they lack today: the chapter navigator, the page-actions surface where it applies, and one settings vocabulary.

## Approach

### The amendment: scope and hard lines

This is the sixth orchestration amendment to the program, recorded alongside the others in [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md). It supersedes the reader row of the seam-depth table in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which read "chrome only, the two engines are separate by design". That ruling predates the program's settlement and was made when chrome sharing was the whole ambition.

- **The host stays one Activity, and that is what makes this surface different.** Every previous takeover put a shared engine above two live per-type models. A host that owns a window, system bars and an orientation lock cannot exist twice, so `ReaderActivity` remains the single host for both content types, keeping the responsibilities only an Activity can own: system bars and insets, cutout padding, orientation, keep-screen-on, custom brightness, the secure flag, the brightness and colour overlay, menu visibility, key and motion dispatch, the loading and error dialogs, and the activity transitions. All of that is already content-neutral in current code.
- **The takeover owns orchestration**: a Reikai-owned reader engine holding chapter navigation, position, app-bar and navigator state, dialog dispatch and the action verbs, dispatching to a provider per content type. The shared chrome composables (`ReaderAppBars`, `ReaderBottomBar`, `ChapterNavigator`, `ReaderPageIndicator`) become content-neutral and move under the engine.
- **`ReaderViewModel` stays live as the manga provider**, minimally patched, and is not manifested. That is this amendment's ruling, not an inherited default: the churn measured above gives no case for absorbing it, and the same clause was ruled the same way for `LibraryViewModel`, `UpdatesViewModel` and `HistoryViewModel`.
- **The engine floor stays Mihon and synced**, never reimplemented: `ChapterLoader` and every `PageLoader`, `DownloadManager`, `SetReadStatus`, `UpsertHistory`, `TrackChapter`, `UpdateChapter`, and every interactor below them. The novel sinks (`SetNovelReadStatus`, `UpsertNovelHistory`, `TrackNovelChapter`, `NovelDownloadManager`, `NovelChapterRepository`) stay likewise. A step that starts reimplementing what `setReadStatus` or `ChapterLoader` does has gone too far.
- **The image viewers are not diverged further.** `PagerViewer`, `WebtoonViewer`, `WebGpuViewer` and their support classes keep porting mechanically, and no step adds a `// RK` marker beyond the five already there (the `seedColor` island in each, plus `WebtoonViewer`'s `volumeScrollDistance` and the two `handleKeyEvent` branches that use it). **One sixth island exists, ruled in by the owner on 2026-09-05**: the chapter-jump fix in `WebGpuViewer.setChapters`, recorded under known defects below. The ruling was for a live user-facing bug found while taking the surface over, and it is not a general licence. The earlier wording, "byte-identical to upstream", was already false for two of the three files and is corrected here rather than quietly kept. `Viewer` itself is not modified either, because the two novel viewers sit behind the engine's neutral contract instead of implementing it; if `Viewer` must grow a member, that is a design decision recorded here first, not an incidental edit.
- **`NovelReaderScreenModel` and the vendored reader dissolve entirely.** Unlike the other surfaces' novel models, this one is not reshaped into a provider: its engine is a WebView plus a vendored `core.js` that is being replaced in **both** rendering modes, so the novel provider is net-new code over the existing novel interactors, and the old tree plus `assets/lnreader-web/` is deleted and manifested. Keeping a WebView mode is not keeping this code.
- **Replaced Mihon files are deleted and manifested in the same commit**, which the `pre-commit` hook enforces. Expect very few rows: almost everything on this surface is engine or host, so the usual delete-and-manifest texture does not apply and most of the change lands as live `// RK` islands instead.
- **Two upstreams from here on.** Tsundoku becomes a second sync source for the two novel viewers and their shared layers only, credited as an Apache-2.0 port the way the Komikku ports are, and recorded in [feature-ports.md](../feature-ports.md) rather than the base ledger. Its row is written when the first port lands with a real SHA, not in advance. `scripts/off-path-check.ps1` is built around one base; extending it is part of the final step, not an assumption.
- **The restore path keeps its integer, and that is deliberate.** Reopening a chapter runs `Chapter.last_page_read` through `ChapterLoader` into `ReaderChapter.requestedPage`, which `PagerViewer`, `WebtoonViewer` and `WebGpuViewer` each read directly when they seek to the restored page. `ChapterLoader` is untouchable engine floor and the image viewers are not to be diverged, so the typed-position rule cannot reach this hop from either end. The resolution is that `requestedPage` stays exactly as it is and serves manga only, `ReaderChapter` gains a typed position beside it, and only the novel viewers read the typed one. Written down because the literal reading of "position is never an `Int`" leads straight into editing three viewers, which is the one thing that turns this takeover back into Option A.

### Ruled design picks (owner, 2026-09-03)

- **Shape C, the neutral host, over the two alternatives.** Folding novels into `ReaderViewModel` by synthesizing manga-shaped rows was rejected: it reintroduces novels-as-manga at the reader boundary, which the program rejected at the data layer for reasons that do not stop being true one layer up. A second Reikai-owned reader host was rejected as the shape that permanently guarantees two of everything, which is what this program exists to stop.
- **The engine owns a neutral viewer contract, and Mihon's `Viewer` is one adapter under it** (owner, 2026-09-03). The earlier framing, that a novel viewer is simply a third implementation of `Viewer`, does not survive contact with the interface. `Viewer.setChapters` takes a `ViewerChapters`, which is three `ReaderChapter`s, and a `ReaderChapter` wraps the manga `Chapter` from `data.database.models` with a secondary constructor over the manga domain `Chapter`. `ReaderPage` extends `Page(index, url, imageUrl, null)` with no text field, and `source-api`'s `Page` has none either. Tsundoku made it fit by adding `var text: String?` to `Page` plus `Source.fetchPageText`, which is exactly the novels-as-manga mechanism this plan rejects. Two other exits were weighed and declined: synthesizing a manga `Chapter` per novel chapter is that same mechanism at one remove, and generalizing `Viewer` / `ViewerChapters` / `ReaderPage` reaches into `source-api`, the contract installed extensions compile against, and forces edits in all three image viewers, which is the Option A tax. The neutral contract keeps the image viewers untouched and matches the program's adapters-are-the-only-seam rule, at the cost of one indirection in the host's viewer slot.
- **Two rendering modes, both from tsundoku, selected by a preference** (owner, 2026-09-03). Native TextView is the default and WebView is the alternative, mirroring tsundoku's own `novelRenderingMode`. The same preference is what makes steps 4 to 8 reachable: it gates the new reader against the old one during the build, defaulting to the old reader, and the old option is removed at the cutover. Tsundoku's two host-coupled viewers (`NovelViewer` 2091 lines, `NovelWebViewViewer` 2876) are **not** taken; our engine drives our own two viewers over their portable layers.
- **Our own reader is not the base, and neither is a from-scratch renderer.** Keeping the LNReader-derived reader was rejected because its engine is the vendored `core.js` nobody can update, which is the whole reason this migration exists. Writing our own was rejected because text rendering is where the hard, already-solved problems are (precomputed text on long chapters, span performance, inline images, justification, CJK utterance splitting), and a from-scratch renderer has no upstream to sync from, which is the core of how Reikai is maintained. Tsundoku is a live Apache-2.0 fork whose portable layers total roughly 3850 lines of Kotlin plus 329 of readable JS, and it wins on both counts.
- **The settings the takeover carries, and the ones it defers** (owner, 2026-09-03). Font size, line height, the four margins, text alignment, font family and theme land inside the renderer lift, because they are the reader's visible identity and they have no UI outside the sheet being deleted. Paragraph indent, paragraph spacing, user regex replacements and custom fonts land too, at the pipeline-and-renderer step rather than the styling one, because indent and spacing are span concerns and regex replacements are a preprocessing concern. That absorbs the four items the roadmap's tsundoku feature-harvest line previously held, so that line narrows to what genuinely remains after the takeover. The full owed-settings inventory, including what silently loses its implementation, is the "Settings the takeover owes" section below.
- **The auto-mark-read threshold stays ours at 97%** (owner, 2026-09-03). It lives today in `NovelReaderScreenModel`'s `saveProgress` as `if (clamped >= 97)`, and the file header documents it; it comes from the LNReader lineage and is the behaviour users already have. Tsundoku's two answers are both declined: their shared `NovelProgress.DONE_THRESHOLD` of `0.99f` and their user-facing `novelMarkAsReadThreshold` slider defaulting to 95. The number moves into the typed position kernel as the novel side's completion predicate, because the file it lives in is deleted.
- **The six settings whose implementation dies with `core.js` are reimplemented, not hidden** (owner, 2026-09-03): bionic reading, remove extra spacing, tap edges to scroll, swipe between chapters, auto-scroll and its speed. Bionic reading is the one with no native equivalent anywhere in the repo, so it is net-new work rather than a re-plumb.
- **The in-reader novel sheet gets string resources** (owner, 2026-09-03). Every label in `NovelReaderSettingsSheet` except the orientation chips and the colour-filter blend modes is a hardcoded English literal today, so the novel in-reader sheet is untranslated. It is done as part of the sheet collapse rather than as a separate pass.
- **Position is a sealed type answered by the provider, and it names its chapter.** Manga answers a page index and a count; novels answer a fraction, which is what `novel_chapters.last_text_progress` already stores as hundredths. The chapter component is not optional detail: because the viewer holds more than one chapter at a time for seamless scrolling, a position that says only "62%" cannot say 62% of what, and the host's existing `onPageSelected(page)` already carries `page.chapter` for exactly this reason. The navigator and indicator read a derived fraction and label scoped to the visible chapter.
- **The novel provider is proven with a deliberately plain viewer before the tsundoku renderer is lifted**, so a host-plumbing failure and a rendering failure cannot arrive together. **The plain viewer's container shape is settled up front, and it was measured rather than assumed.** It is a `RecyclerView` of chapters whose item view is the renderer's chunk stack. That is the one shape that gets both halves right, and an earlier draft of this doc got it wrong in both directions. Tsundoku uses a scroll view over a linear layout of appended chapters, which is exactly why they need a compensating scroll when they prune: nothing recycles there, so every loaded chapter stays laid out. A recycler lays out only the visible chapter, so a chapter inserted above the reader costs nothing and moves nothing. Their `ChapterTextBlock` is already a linear layout of chunk views, so it drops into a recycler item unchanged and the renderer port pays nothing for this. Verified in `RecyclerPrependPositionTest`; the drift that does exist is described under "Known defects". The plain viewer is that container from the start, holding plain text with no spans, no images and one chapter, and every later step adds into it rather than replacing it.
- **Our TTS transport is kept and tsundoku's text handling is taken.** Measured rather than assumed: we have a `MediaSessionCompat` with lock-screen and headset transport that tsundoku has nowhere in its tree, and tsundoku has an utterance splitter capped at the engine's maximum input length with a no-spaces branch for CJK, which we lack entirely. Neither side requests audio focus, ducks, or has a sleep timer, and neither highlights below paragraph granularity, so the roadmap's in-text-highlight item survives this migration rather than being delivered by it.
- **Seamless chapter-to-chapter scrolling comes from Mihon's mechanism, not tsundoku's.** The host already spans chapters: `WebtoonAdapter.setChapters` builds one item list from the previous, current and next chapters' pages with `ChapterTransition` items between them, `WebtoonViewer` calls `requestPreloadChapter` as a boundary approaches, and `onPageSelected` reports which chapter is now visible through `ReaderPage.chapter`. A text viewer rendering `ViewerChapters` the same way inherits all of it, so novels get the capability from the engine rather than from a second implementation. **Tsundoku's own infinite-scroll machinery is deliberately not ported:** it lives inside their host-coupled `NovelViewer` (a `loadedChapters` list, their own append path, threshold auto-load, cross-chapter progress math, and four `prepare*ChapterForInfiniteScroll` methods on their forked `ReaderViewModel`), it is gated off by default there, and porting it would rebuild what the host already provides while dragging in the fork this takeover exists to avoid. **The decline was drawn one file too wide, and is narrowed here.** `ChapterQueue` (their unbounded append and prepend list with trimming) is genuinely replaced by Mihon's three-chapter `ViewerChapters` and stays declined. `NovelProgress` is not: it is 48 lines of renderer-agnostic arithmetic in `shared/` with no host dependency, and it holds rules the position kernel and the seam step both need anyway. `forwardChaptersToMarkRead(oldIndex, newIndex, size)` is the "a fling scrolled past whole chapters, mark them read" rule, `NEXT_LOAD_RETRY_COOLDOWN_MS` (15s) stops a chapter that keeps failing at the boundary from respawning a request every scroll frame, and `progressToPercent` rounds rather than truncates so a stored position matches between renderers. Its own KDoc, "the slider now seeks only within the current chapter", independently corroborates the chapter-scoped position ruling above. Take it.
- **The settings restructure ships first, before the reader work starts.** It is independent of the migration, it is the visible answer to `unseensnick/Reikai#55`, and it is the one piece a user notices immediately.

- **The two viewers are rebuilt against our architecture; only the layers beneath them are ported** (owner, 2026-09-03, on the condition that a port must integrate rather than bolt on). A third research round measured the seam precisely and it falls below the viewers, not through them. Their forward-only chapter queue is referenced 76 times in the native viewer and 39 in the WebView one, and **zero times in any portable layer**, so it is the viewers' spine rather than a detail. Keeping it would mean running a second chapter-window model beside Mihon's, which is the derive-or-do-not-own defect the program exists to stop; removing it means editing the viewers' core loop at 115 sites, which is a rebuild wearing a port's clothes. Their viewers also consume a text field on `ReaderPage`, which needs the same field on `source-api`'s `Page`, the contract installed extensions compile against. Ruling backward scrolling in (below) settles it, because their model cannot express it at all.
- **Seamless scrolling goes both ways, matching the webtoon viewer** (owner, 2026-09-03). Tsundoku is forward-only and the backward half of their code is dead, so this half has no reference implementation and is net-new work. See the prepend trap under "Known defects" for why the webtoon viewer's own mechanism does not transfer either.
- **The engine's chapter window is its own type, not `ViewerChapters`.** `NovelSource.parseChapter` returns a raw HTML body with no page concept anywhere in the novel source contract, and a downloaded chapter is a single self-contained file with its images inlined. So a novel chapter cannot travel as a `ReaderPage`, and the earlier wording "renders `ViewerChapters` across previous, current and next" is not literally achievable. The engine builds its own three-slot window the way `WebtoonAdapter` builds its item list, over novel chapters as HTML, and Mihon's `ViewerChapters` stays the manga adapter's own type. This is decided at the engine step, not discovered at the seam step.
- **The font manager ships, both halves** (owner, 2026-09-03). Import and in-app download from Google Fonts, ported from tsundoku with three fixes, because import alone would send the user out to a browser and back for every font. Reject woff and woff2 at download time: their validator accepts five formats while their lister shows only TrueType and OpenType, and Android cannot build a typeface from woff2 at all, so a download can currently land as a file the app then hides. Resolve the manager from the graph rather than constructing a second one in the settings screen, which today gives two typeface caches. Keep their curated 29-family list, which includes four CJK families that matter here, so no Google Fonts API key is needed. Storage needs one new directory beside downloads and novel downloads under the user's existing tree, and no new permission.
- **The TTS splitter is built on `BreakIterator`, not ported** (owner, 2026-09-03). Tsundoku's is ASCII-only in its sentence set so CJK terminators never match, hard-cuts mid-word every 4000 characters when the text has no spaces, decides that from a single boolean over the whole text so one Latin sentence flips the strategy for a Japanese chapter, and has a fallback that can emit a chunk longer than the cap. `BreakIterator` is in neither repository today; its sentence instance is locale-correct and its word and line instances use dictionary segmentation for Chinese, Japanese, Thai and Khmer, which is the case tsundoku hard-cuts.
- **Chapter translation and EPUB export are parked** (owner, 2026-09-03), and are not part of this takeover. Translation is roughly 2400 lines across three modules with twelve engines, off by default, and hard-wired to their forked view model. EPUB export is not reachable from the reader at all in their app. Both go to the roadmap's parked section with a revive trigger rather than into the sequence.

### What this must not become

**Option A was built here and reverted.** A single Compose shell hosting both mediums, with the image viewers decoupled from `ReaderActivity` behind a `ViewerHost` interface and embedded through `AndroidView`, was built across two commits and reverted. The record in [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md) is explicit that the revert was cost, not feasibility: an on-device spike confirmed the webtoon view scrolled and zoomed correctly inside `AndroidView`. The cost was that it diverged the hot image-viewer files from upstream, creating a permanent hand-port tax, and ran modified viewers even on the fallback path.

That decline does not transfer to this takeover, and the reason is precise enough to be a test: Option A's tax came from decoupling the **viewers**, and this takeover neutralizes the **orchestration** above them while leaving the viewers untouched. That is the same line every previous amendment drew. It also means the decline has not expired so much as been routed around, and the routing only holds while the viewers-are-never-modified rule holds. The moment a step edits a viewer, this surface has become Option A and the revert's reasoning applies again in full.

### Known defects and traps in the surfaces being replaced

Recorded so the takeover fixes them deliberately rather than carrying them across, each naming the step that owns it.

- **Five places assume an integer page index** (step 2). The chapter navigator builds its slider as `steps = totalPages - 2` over `valueRange = 1f..totalPages` in `ChapterNavigator`, so a chapter modelled as one stub page produces negative steps. This does not throw, and the earlier wording overstated it: M3's `SliderState` class carries no `require`, the check lives in the `Slider` composable itself, and the navigator composes that only behind a `totalPages > 1` guard, which the vertical path repeats as `showSlider`. The real defect is therefore a silent capability loss, a one-stub-page chapter gets no seekbar at all, and step 2 owes novels a real scrubbing range rather than a crash fix. `moveToPageIndex` resolves a page by list index. `updateChapterProgress` persists `lastPageRead` as an index and fires completion only when `pages.lastIndex == pageIndex`, so a fraction-driven viewer would never mark a chapter read. Download-ahead uses the ratio `page.number / pages.size`. Restoring after process death assigns `requestedPage` an index.
- **A long paragraph is silently truncated when read aloud** (step 8). `SystemTtsEngine.speak` passes the whole paragraph to `TextToSpeech.speak` with no length check, and the platform drops text past its maximum input length. Tsundoku's splitter is the fix and arrives with the renderer.
- **`MainActivity` implements the reader's `NovelVolumeKeyHost`** (step 9, and it cannot be earlier), a compile-level dependency of a Mihon file on the novel reader package. `MainActivity` implements the interface because the current novel reader is a Voyager screen hosted there, and `NovelReaderScreen` registers the handler while it is on screen, so the interface can only be removed once that screen is deleted. Routing volume keys through the viewer's own `handleKeyEvent` is a separate, earlier job that lands at step 4: `WebtoonViewer` already does exactly that, reading a user-settable `volumeScrollDistance` inside `handleKeyEvent` under a `// RK` island marked as novel parity, so the mechanism exists and only needs a second caller.
- **The TTS transport has exactly one owner and it is being deleted** (step 8). `NovelTtsSession`'s callbacks are written only by `NovelTtsController`, which is constructed only by `NovelReaderScreenModel`. Replacing the model compiles clean and leaves the lock-screen and notification buttons as silent no-ops.
- **The reader settings suffix carried search breadcrumbs** (resolved in step 1, kept here as the record). `contentTypedCategory` existed so a duplicated setting read distinctly and its settings-search breadcrumb differed per content type, which made retiring it a search regression risk rather than a cosmetic change. Step 1 took that risk and cleared it: the helper still has twelve call sites elsewhere in settings, but neither reader screen uses it any more. Nothing outstanding.
- **Nothing on our side of this surface is tested** (every step). No test class touches the novel reader, nothing under any reader path has a test at all, and the repo has no Compose UI tests, so every check below is a device check or a new unit test written against a kernel that did not previously exist. **The imported half is different and that is a reason to import it:** roughly 2400 lines of unit tests sit over exactly the layers being ported, and zero over the two viewers being left behind. Those tests come across with the code.
- **The novel viewers have no dispatch point, and tsundoku's answer is wrong for us** (step 4). `ReadingMode.toViewer` is keyed off `ReadingMode`, which has no novel member, so nothing currently constructs a text viewer. Tsundoku added a `NOVEL` member to the enum and branches inside `toViewer`. Copying that leaks a "Novel" option into the manga reading-mode UI at five call sites (both dropdowns on the manga settings screen, the in-reader reading-mode dialog, the general settings page and the reading-mode page), and one of those slices the list positionally with `entries.drop(1)`, so inserting anywhere but the end silently changes what is dropped. The engine dispatches on content type before it reaches `toViewer` instead.
- **A chapter picked from the chapter list never reached the screen** (found and fixed while taking the surface over). `WebGpuViewer.setChapters` read `currentPage ?: getPage(requestedPage)`, and `currentPage` is null only on the very first call, so every later one discarded the incoming chapter's requested page. The model, the app bar and the page count moved to the new chapter while the viewer kept rendering the old one, which reads as the tap doing nothing. Measured on device before and after: the jump showed 30 pages and saved a Chapter 5 page, and a viewer rebuild alone turned that into 43 pages and a Chapter 2 page. The fix keeps the anchor only when the page on screen belongs to the newly current chapter, which is what separates a jump from scrolling across a boundary: `loadNewChapter` reaches the same method during a seamless crossing, and there the page on screen already belongs to the new chapter, so it still wins and the reader does not lurch. Verified both ways, including a backward crossing landing at the previous chapter's last page rather than its first. **The same line exists verbatim in `refs/mihon`, but the reader's chapter list is Reikai's, so upstream may have no route to it.** The next-chapter button reaches the same call and was measured working before the fix, so what separates the two paths is not established; do not assume the button was ever broken.
- **Menu visibility is host state that lives in the manga provider, and the image viewers are why it cannot simply move** (step 4 or 5, needs a ruling before it does). The amendment assigns menu visibility to the host, but it sits in `ReaderViewModel.State` today and the three image viewers read it directly at ten sites to gate long-tap and volume keys (`PagerViewer.kt:127,397,404`, `WebtoonViewer.kt:95,131,319,331`, `WebGpuViewer.kt:850,1535,1543`). A field read cannot be intercepted by a forwarder, so relocating the state means editing all three, which adds markers past the five the hard line freezes them at, `WebGpuViewer` having none today. Nothing breaks in the meantime, because the image viewers only run in a manga session. The writes are not the problem: `toggleMenu`, `showMenu` and `hideMenu` (`ReaderActivity.kt:785-805`) are already Activity methods the viewers call, so they absorb into `ReaderHost` for free. This is the one place where "the host owns menu visibility" and "the image viewers are never diverged further" cannot both hold, and the step that moves it owes the owner that choice rather than making it in passing.
- **`EntryId` has to cross an Intent boundary** (step 4). `ReaderActivity.newIntent` takes a nullable `mangaId` and puts it in a `"manga"` extra alongside `"chapter"`, `"page_index"` and Reikai's `"source_scoped"`. Identity in the shared layer is the sealed `EntryId`, which an Intent extra cannot hold directly, so how it is encoded and decoded is a step 4 decision rather than something to improvise at the call site.
- **Two novel-reader settings are only editable on the manga screen** (step 11). The current novel reader reads `ReaderPreferences.verticalNavigatorHeight` and `verticalNavigatorOnLeft` for its progress rail, but both rows live on the manga reader screen. The sheet collapse is where that gets a per-type answer.
- **Tsundoku ships four dead or unwired novel preferences** (step 6, as a do-not-port list). `novelKeepChaptersLoaded` and `novelGlobalPresets` have only their declarations, `novelCustomCss` and `novelCustomJs` are read but have no writer since the snippet lists superseded them, and `novelTapToScroll` has a settings row that no viewer reads. The "Theme null" row this bullet used to name as a fourth item was already fixed upstream when the bullet was written, in tsundoku `c4e87b492`, so it is struck rather than carried.
- **Two of tsundoku's preference reactions rebuild the whole document** (step 6). Their observer routes paragraph indent, paragraph spacing and text-selectable through a full `reloadContent`, which clears every view and re-fetches, re-preprocesses and re-parses every loaded chapter. Indent and spacing are applied purely as spans over an already-built `SpannableStringBuilder` and nothing in the parse depends on them; text-selectable only switches the `PrecomputedText` path plus three view properties. This is the same class as Reikai's known `generalSettings` DOM-rebuild trap, so the wiring is not inherited as-is. Related and worth checking on device rather than assuming: their font size sits in the live-refresh bucket, but the renderer bakes it into the spacing and indent pixel values, so those spans look like they stay stale until the next reload.

- **Drift is a within-chapter problem, not a between-chapter one** (steps 5 and 6). This was measured rather than reasoned, in `RecyclerPrependPositionTest`, after an earlier draft of this doc asserted the opposite and was wrong. Inserting a chapter above the reader moves nothing, even when that chapter then grows by five and a half screens, because a recycler never lays out an item that is entirely above the viewport and the layout manager anchors from the visible child. What does move the reader is a chunk **inside the visible chapter, above the reading position**, finishing its measurement: the displacement is exactly the growth. Growth below the reading position is invisible. So compensation belongs inside the chapter view when a chunk re-measures, which is what an asynchronous precomputed-text application or a font-size change causes, and the seamless step needs none of it. The measurements are in the test's logcat output under tag `PrependSpike`.
- **Backward preload has one narrow trigger** (step 5). The webtoon viewer preloads backward only when an upward scroll leaves a `Prev` transition item first-visible, and in the steady seamless case no transition item exists at all. There is no page-count-based backward heuristic to copy, so a text viewer needs its own trigger.
- **A single-page chapter marks itself read on entry** (step 4, and the reason novels must not travel as pages). Completion is `pages.lastIndex == pageIndex`, so with one page it is `0 == 0` immediately: the chapter is marked read, trackers sync and it can be deleted the moment the reader opens. Download-ahead also fires unconditionally because the ratio is `1.0` against a `0.25` threshold, and `requestedPage` can only ever be zero, so intra-chapter restore is structurally impossible. Tsundoku hit this and patched around it, with a comment in their own view model saying that for novel chapters marking complete via page index would always fire on selection.
- **`startPostponedEnterTransition` has exactly one call site in the whole reader package** (step 4), inside `updateViewer`. A path that reaches the screen without running through that method leaves the postponed enter transition unstarted, so the user sees a blank reader with no error. The surrounding code says it waits for the enter transition to avoid a crash on API 26, so it is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.
- **One saved-state key does two jobs** (step 2). `page_index` is both the launch extra Reikai added for the gallery page-preview jump and the key the view model reads and writes for process-death restore. A fractional position breaks both at once and breaks them silently, because a wrong-typed read returns the default and falls through to the stored page. **Resolved in step 2:** the launch jump moved to its own `launch_page` key and both sources are now consumed once, by the chapter they were meant for.
- **The restore index leaks between chapters** (step 2). `requestedPage` is re-applied to every chapter that becomes current rather than only the first, so a position from the previous chapter carries into the next and is saved only by the viewers' clamp against the last index. **Resolved in step 2**, and it was broader than recorded here: the index is rewritten on every progress save, so after the first page turn of a session every chapter opened afterwards inherited the previous one.
- **The host is coupled to the manga model as a concrete type at roughly 90 sites** (step 3), and its state, dialog and event types appear in composable signatures. Measured 2026-09-04, correcting an earlier "41 places" that matches no count that can be reproduced: `viewModel.` appears 59 times in `ReaderActivity` plus 13 method references, with 33 lines reading its state. Seven viewer files reach the model through the Activity at 17 sites, not four files at six, and the viewers touch 14 distinct Activity members in all. Two ordering hazards sit exactly where the takeover cuts, and there are three rather than two: a documented one where custom info and the merge group must resolve before the state update that builds the viewer, and two undocumented ones, that `manga -> updateViewer` must register before `viewerChapters -> setChapters` because the latter reads the viewer the former installs, and that the view model's own collector must register before both, which holds only because the Activity touches the model before registering its own.
- **A chapter cannot be one TextView** (step 6). Tsundoku splits into views of at least 6000 characters, cutting after the next newline so a chunk never ends mid-paragraph, and their class documentation gives the reason: with one view, layout, span lookup and selection hit-testing are all order-of-chapter, and large chapters caused constant garbage collection and multi-second touch handling. **This is also where the reader can actually be displaced.** A chunk finishing its measurement above the reading position moves the reader by exactly its growth, measured in `RecyclerPrependPositionTest`, so applying precomputed text or a style change to an already-visible chapter owes a compensating scroll. Growth below the reading position is free. Other costs to inherit knowingly: a selection cannot span a chunk boundary, an image span's owning view is found by scanning every chunk, and precomputed text is used only when selection is off, each application guarded against a metrics-mismatch throw.
- **Our TTS stalls permanently on an oversized paragraph** (step 8, and a live bug today rather than a migration concern). There is no length guard anywhere on the path. The engine passes the text straight to the platform and discards the return value; the listener does handle error callbacks, but when the text exceeds the maximum input length the platform returns an error synchronously without ever invoking the listener, so the pending completion callback is never cleared and playback hangs with the notification stuck on Playing. Two independent fixes, both small: complete on an error return, which turns a stall into a skipped paragraph, and split before speaking so oversized text never reaches the engine.

### Settings the takeover owes

**Why this inventory is one-sided, stated rather than implied** (owner, 2026-09-05). Write-once says cost and differing structure are not exits and only a named mechanism is, so the mechanism is named here once: the typographic settings below (font size and family, line height, margins, alignment, indent, paragraph spacing, reader themes, custom fonts, regex replacements) act on text, and a manga page is an image the source ships, so there is no text for them to act on. That is a property of the content, not of the engines, and it is what makes them novel-only rather than novel-first. Anything in this section that is **not** text-shaped does not get that exemption and is scoped by type like any other change.

The plan originally had no settings dimension, which is where the user-visible regression sits: the app-level screens were restructured in step 1, but nothing said what happens to the settings whose *implementation* is inside the tree being deleted. This section is that inventory. Every row is carried or dropped, nothing is deferred to another item, and an unmarked row means the takeover is not finished.

**Implementation and only UI both gone (carried, at the renderer step).** Font size, line height, side padding, text alignment, font family, background colour and text colour reach the page only as `--readerSettings-*` CSS custom properties written by `NovelReaderHtmlBuilder` and rewritten live by `core.js`, and they are surfaced only in the in-reader sheet. Deleting the tree removes the implementation and the sole UI in one commit while the preference keys survive in the store, so a user would lose font choice and reader themes outright with nothing left on the app-level screen to hint they existed. The follow-system-theme toggle rides along, since it only resolves into the last two. Bundled fonts load through a `FontFace` from `file:///android_asset/fonts/`; a native renderer loads them with `FontFamily(Font(path, assetManager))`, which the current sheet already does for its own chip previews. Note that padding is a single value today and becomes tsundoku's four separate margins.

**Row survives, implementation dies (carried, reimplemented).** Bionic reading, remove extra spacing, tap edges to scroll, swipe between chapters, auto-scroll and auto-scroll speed all keep their rows on the app-level novel screen and keep toggling after the deletion with nothing behind them. Bionic reading is the loudest, because it is an accessibility row, a sheet toggle and a selectable bottom-bar button at once, and `textVide` has no native equivalent anywhere in the repo, so it is net-new work rather than a re-plumb. Two TTS placement options go the same way and are carried on the same terms: auto page advance and scroll to top.

**Infrastructure every setting above sits on (carried, at the plumbing step).** These are not settings but nothing works without them, and all four are WebView-only today. The injected scroll listener plus `core.js` reports progress, and that one channel drives the percentage overlay, the seekbar, resume position and the 97% auto-mark-read. The centre-tap chrome toggle is a `core.js` message. The scroll handles that volume-key paging and rail scrubbing call into are `evaluateJavascript`. And TTS paragraph enumeration is entirely `core.js`'s `tts` object driven by five one-line JS calls, so read-aloud does not work at all until a renderer owns paragraph enumeration, which is why the TTS step stays after the renderer step.

**Newly possible, carried (owner, 2026-09-03).** Paragraph indent and paragraph spacing, which have no preference today and are hardcoded margins in the vendored CSS. User regex find-and-replace rules. Custom fonts through a font manager. And the rendering-mode picker itself, which is a new setting rather than a replacement for one. The first four were previously held by the roadmap's tsundoku feature-harvest line, which no longer exists as a separate item.

**Survives untouched.** Orientation, both the global default and the per-novel `viewer_flags` override. Keep screen on. The whole brightness and colour-filter block, already a native shared `ReaderContentOverlay` outside the tree. Skip duplicate chapters, mark read on skip, bottom-bar button selection, incognito behaviour, and download-ahead.

**Dropped.** The two floating-TTS-puck position keys become orphans when the puck is deleted, and they are state rather than options, so nothing replaces them.

**Carried, but last (owner, 2026-09-03).** Tsundoku's reading status bar and its reorderable elements screen, the customizable in-reader bottom bar, in-reader text edit mode, quotes, the font manager with both import and download, CSS and JS snippet editors, per-chapter auto-split, and their `novelMarkShortChapterAsRead`. None of these is required for parity with what novels have today, so they land after the cutover rather than gating it, but they belong to this takeover rather than to a follow-up item: the roadmap line that used to hold them is gone, and their step is below. They come before the sheet collapse deliberately, because collapsing the sheets around a settings set that is about to grow would mean laying it out twice.

**Parked, not part of this takeover (owner, 2026-09-03).** Chapter translation and EPUB export. Translation is roughly 2400 lines across three modules, twelve engines, off by default and wired into their forked view model; EPUB export is not reachable from their reader at all. Both keep a roadmap line with a revive trigger.

**The parity direction, for the record.** Tsundoku's in-reader novel page is 2010 lines against our 583, and their app-level novel screen 445 against our 140, across five in-reader tabs to our three. The takeover is not chasing that number for its own sake. The bar it is held to is that nothing a Reikai user has today disappears; the newly-possible group and the extras group are what close most of the remaining gap, and what stays unported is recorded in the do-not-port note above rather than left implicit.

That is step 11's settings collapse, and the dying sheet is the only UI that writes the novel copies today.

**The sanitiser is a rendering cleanup, not a security boundary, and nothing should describe it as one.** Verified bypasses: `</script >` with a space is a valid end tag the regex misses; comments are stripped after scripts and the result is never re-scanned, so `<scr<!--x-->ipt>` splices back into a live tag; and attributes are untouched, so `onerror`, `javascript:` URLs and `iframe` all survive. Compounding it, `loadDataWithBaseURL` takes the plugin's own declared `site` as the document origin and the WebView shares one cookie jar with the tracker logins. A real fix is a parser-based allowlist, which is step 7's business since that step owns the embedded-CSS and JS settings.

**The sanitiser's cost was measured and needs no fix, correcting an earlier note that it did.** A 4.8 MB chapter (six base64-inlined images, the shape a downloaded chapter takes) sanitises in 47 ms on the JVM at the default settings, once per chapter, inside `viewModelScope.launchIO`, with the next chapter prefetched into `htmlCache` ahead of the user. The claim that its five `Regex.replace` passes each copy the whole string is false: Android's `Matcher.replaceAll` returns the input instance when nothing matched, and removing every comment from the fixture moved 47.2 ms to 45.6 ms, so the copies are 3% of it and the linear scans are the rest. Guarding each pass with a `contains` check therefore trades one scan for another and buys nothing. The one genuinely wasteful path is the Jsoup round-trip that strips `style` attributes, 59 ms of the 106 ms measured with embedded CSS off, and it is left alone deliberately: guarding it on the attribute name changes the output for malformed markup Jsoup would have repaired, and a guard loose enough to be safe fires on any chapter containing the word style.

**Step 6 has started, seam first and pipeline second.** The viewer contract gained `TextViewport`, a capability carrying `load` and `applySettings`, which the host asks for with `as?` instead of casting to the concrete WebView class; the chapter document moved into that viewport, since the HTML is its own format, and the host kept only the theme resolve, which needs its night mode. Then the two readers' byte-identical `loadChapterHtml` twins became one session-scoped `NovelChapterTextLoader`, because the pipeline had to land once rather than fork. The pipeline itself is ported into `reikai/novel/content/` and runs inside that loader at `RenderTarget.WEB_VIEW`, so both readers get it: seven stages, the eight preferences on `ln_reader_*` keys, and a Chapter text settings group for the six that have a UI. **The markers their HTML carries are renamed** from `data-tsundoku-*` to `data-reikai-*`, which the WebView port at step 7 must match. **Two stages ship inert:** regex replacements, whose rule-list editor is its own slice, and markdown, which `detectTextKind` reaches only from a `.md` chapter URL that no current source produces. **The one behaviour change to know about** is that `sanitizeForRender` strips scripts by default, so a chapter's own scripts no longer run in either reader. Verified on the emulator by turning force-lowercase on and off around a chapter open, which is the check that could fail: the body changed and the chrome title did not.

**The native renderer is in as a third rendering mode, and all three work.** `NovelTextViewport` implements the same `TextViewport` capability the WebView one does, so the host installs whichever the preference names and nothing above the seam knows the difference; `novelReaderTarget` sends both shared-host modes to the same Activity, which the compiler forced by making its `when` non-exhaustive. Ported into `reikai/presentation/reader/text/`: the renderer, the 6000-character chunking, the paragraph spans and the link-only movement method, re-plumbed off their host Activity and preference class onto a `Context` and the settings the session already resolved. The styling layer is net-new, as the scout predicted: `NovelTextStyle` applies size, line spacing, alignment, padding, colour and the nine bundled faces through `Typeface.createFromAsset`, replacing the `file:///android_asset` font loading that dies with `core.js`. **Two things the device found that the port did not.** Progress must not be reported before the text is measured: a recycler with no scroll range reported 100%, which at 97% marks the chapter read and retires its download, so an unmeasured range now reports 0 and a chapter shorter than the screen does too, matching what the WebView reports for one. And the renderer takes the HTML built for a WebView sink rather than the pipeline's `NovelChapterContent`, because `LoadedChapter` carries only the escaped string; opening that seam is what lets the native path skip the HTML round-trip, and it belongs with the settings slice. **Still owed by this step:** images (the image getter and its Referer plumbing), text selection, links, and the net-new settings, which are paragraph indent, paragraph spacing, the four margins that replace our single padding, custom fonts and the regex-replacement editor.

**Links work in the native renderer, text selection does not yet, and the two are exclusive.** Tsundoku wires `LinkOnlyMovementMethod` only when selection is off and `ArrowKeyMovementMethod` when it is on (`NovelViewer.kt:735-745`), so a link is dead in their default configuration too; the choice is a preference, not a gap. Ours is `ln_reader_text_selectable`, and it is **off by default and has no settings row yet**, because the selectable branch does not work: with `setTextIsSelectable(true)` a long press produces no action mode and no highlight, and the chunk text also drops out of the accessibility tree. The suspicion is the recycler claiming the gesture before the long-press timeout, which would mean custom touch handling rather than a flag. Nothing advertises selection until it does something. **What the link branch cost, found only by running it:** the chunk views cover the whole chapter, so a tap on text never reached the item view and the chrome could not be toggled at all, leaving no way to the toolbar. The tap listener moved onto the chunk views, which works because `LinkOnlyMovementMethod` declines a tap that is not on a link. Verified on device: an external link opens the browser and leaves the reader in place, an `intent://` and a `javascript:` link are both refused with the reader never losing focus, and a centre tap raises the full bar.

**The image getter is ported and fetching, but its span sizing is unfinished.** `NovelImageGetter` drops tsundoku's host coupling entirely rather than re-plumbing it: their Activity becomes a `Context`, and the Referer comes from the chapter's own base URL, which the session already resolved and the viewport already holds, so no page loader or source lookup is needed. Their `tsundoku-novel-image://` branch is not taken, because it serves an archive our content never produces; a downloaded chapter carries its images inline as data URLs, which is the offline path here. Verified on device that both halves reach the network and the decoder: a chapter image returns 200 through OkHttp and Coil reports a successful decode, and an inline data URL decodes with a sample size matched to the column width. **Sizing needed the precomputed layout rebuilt, not a `requestLayout`.** A `PrecomputedText` caches the measurement it was built from, so changing a drawable's bounds afterwards cannot affect it and asking for a layout re-uses the stale metrics; that is why tsundoku rebuilds it, and doing the same fixed it. The rebuild is the renderer's job rather than the getter's, because only the renderer knows whether the text is precomputed at all, and a selectable view has no precomputed layout so a plain `requestLayout` is right there. Verified with two images of known shape: a 600x200 and a 400x400 both render full width at their true aspect, in order, with the chapter text between them. **What is left is vertical spacing, not size:** each image sits below a gap of several blank lines, which is how `Html.fromHtml` lays out an image span inside its own paragraph rather than anything the getter does.

**Step 5 was scouted 2026-09-06 and the order changed, along with three claims in this doc.** **The container line in step 4 was stale**, still describing tsundoku's scroll view after the spike had settled on a recycler; it is marked superseded there rather than deleted, because it is the shape the lift starts from. **Steps 5 and 6 swap** for the reason recorded in step 5. **The backward half has no reference implementation to port, which the old wording implied it had.** Tsundoku's native viewer never calls `prepend` at all, and their webview viewer's complete prepend path (`NovelWebViewViewer.kt:1252`, `prependHtmlContent` at `:1287`) is reachable only when `isInfiniteScrollPrepend` is true, which is declared false at `:140`, read at `:1108`, reset at `:1109` and assigned true nowhere in their tree. Their seamless scrolling is also opt-in and off by default (`ReaderPreferences.kt:302`), forward-triggered at 95% of the chapter (`:363`). Ours is on by default, since manga's webtoon reader has no such switch and parity is the default (owner, 2026-09-06), and it keeps the 97% completion rule already ruled here rather than `NovelProgress`'s own 0.99 (owner, same date), taking that file's other arithmetic whole.

**How the position is held is a design choice, and compensation is the fallback rather than the plan** (owner asked for the proper route, 2026-09-06). Tsundoku compensates by hand, `scrollView.scrollBy(0, -removedHeight)` (`NovelViewer.kt:1642`), because a scroll view holds its offset and has no anchoring; a recycler's layout manager anchors on a visible child, which is why the spike measured 2px or less on a prepend and why none of that compensation is inherited. What the spike did find is growth **inside** the anchored item, and there are two structural answers to it rather than an arithmetic one. **Make heights final before layout**, which the text already does through `PrecomputedTextCompat` (`NovelTextRenderer.kt:101,117`); the residual source is images, which render a fixed 200dp placeholder (`NovelImageGetter.kt:77-81`) and then swap to the drawable's true height (`:195-202`), so reserving the real box up front removes the largest remaining shift. **Or make the chunk the item rather than the chapter**, so the anchor sits beside the reading position and growth above it lands in a different item; that is the more correct structure and it costs the re-plumbing of `ChapterTextBlock`'s chunk ownership, which TTS offset mapping, translation re-render and selection all index into. Reserve height first and measure before paying for the second. **The WebView mode needs neither**: Chromium's own scroll anchoring is on by default and the WebView inherits it, so the trap there is correcting manually and fighting it.

`RecyclerPrependPositionTest` measured the case the plan was worried about and found no problem: a chapter inserted above the reader displaces nothing, even when it later grows by more than five screens. The displacement that does exist is inside the visible chapter, when a chunk above the reading position finishes measuring, and it is exactly the growth. That moves the risk from the seamless step to the renderer step, where it is a known quantity, and it settled the container shape as a recycler of chapters over chunk stacks. Two claims in earlier revisions of this doc were wrong and are corrected above rather than quietly dropped.

The measurements behind the ruling were taken 2026-09-03 against the synced base: upstream churn as quoted under Why, our divergence at 16 and 54 `// RK` markers on the two host files, the novel reader at 3155 lines of Kotlin plus 793 of vendored `core.js` and two minified third-party bundles, and the app-level reader settings screen at 68 rows before step 1 split it.

Tsundoku's side, measured the same day: the text package at 3153 lines of which 2091 are its host-coupled `NovelViewer` and 960 the portable renderer; the webview package at 4040 of which 2876 are its host-coupled viewer and about 1160 portable, over 329 lines of their own JS; the `shared/` package at 1621. So roughly 3850 lines of Kotlin plus 329 of JS are portable, and their two viewers, 4967 lines, are what our engine replaces. Their in-reader novel settings page is 2010 lines to our 583, and their app-level novel screen 445 to our 140.

## Decisions & tradeoffs

- **The host is one Activity and the engine sits above two providers.** The program's usual shape does not survive contact with a window-owning host, and pretending it does is how this surface would get mis-planned.
- **Not diverging the image viewers further is what keeps this from being Option A**, and it is the first thing to check when a step feels like it is fighting the design. The rule is now stated as a marker count rather than "byte-identical", because two of the three files already carry markers and a rule the codebase visibly breaks is a rule nobody can apply.
- **Position is typed, not an `Int`, with one named exception.** Sharing an integer page count between a paged medium and a continuous one is the derive-or-do-not-own defect in its most tempting form: the storage would be shared and the meaning forked. The exception is `ReaderChapter.requestedPage`, and it is an exception precisely because honouring the rule there would mean editing the three image viewers, which costs more than it buys.
- **The tsundoku port is the two viewers' portable layers, not their reader.** Their `NovelViewer` calls twenty-one distinct members of their forked `ReaderActivity`, and their host carries roughly fifty `is NovelViewer || is NovelWebViewViewer` type checks, so taking either viewer whole would mean taking their fork of the file we are least willing to fork. That type-check sprawl is also the concrete cost of the design Shape C was chosen over, and it is worth reading before anyone proposes instanceof branching in the host again. The renderer, the WebView stylers and the shared pipeline underneath are domain-free and are the parts worth having.
- **Their engine, not ours and not a new one.** Keeping the LNReader-derived reader was rejected because its engine is a vendored file nobody can update, which is the reason the migration exists. A from-scratch renderer was rejected because it has no upstream to sync from, and text rendering is where the already-solved hard problems are. This is the same maintainability-via-upstream argument that justifies the Mihon base.
- **The seam falls below their viewers, not through them**, and that is what keeps this a port rather than a bolt-on. The measurement decided it: their forward-only chapter queue appears 115 times across the two viewers and not once in any layer beneath them. Below that line the code is domain-free and tested; above it, it is welded to a data model where novels are manga rows.
- **Their reader is a source, not a base.** Tsundoku has no novel tables at all, so everything of theirs that touches persistence encodes novels-as-manga, which Reikai rejected at the data layer. That is not a flaw in their app, it is a different decision, and it is the reason the port stops where it does.
- **The WebView mode is a capability slot, not a hedge.** A text layout cannot render a source's own CSS faithfully, which is a named mechanism, so the mode exists for the sources that need it rather than as insurance against the native renderer failing.
- **We keep our TTS transport even though we are taking their engine.** The two halves were measured separately, and each side won one.
- **Accepted sync cost.** This is the highest-churn surface the program has taken over, on the least diverged files, and the amendment says so rather than borrowing the library's sunk-tax argument. Every future upstream reader fix becomes a manifest-guarded triage on the orchestration we own, while the viewers and the engine floor keep porting mechanically, which is the boundary that keeps the cost bounded.
- **The in-reader sheet collapse waits for the cutover and for the extras** (step 11) rather than leading, because folding novels into the host changes what the two sheets are. The app-level settings screen did not wait, because it was orthogonal, and it shipped as step 1.
- **The settings inventory is part of the takeover, not a follow-up.** A takeover is judged by what the replaced code did, and on this surface a large part of what it did was implement settings inside a vendored JS file. Leaving that to a separate roadmap item would have shipped a reader that silently lost font choice and themes, which is the exact failure the behaviour-inventory bar exists to catch.
- **The mark-read threshold is the incumbent 97%, not tsundoku's number and not a new preference.** Changing a threshold nobody complained about would be a behaviour change smuggled in under a migration, and adding a slider for it is a preference nobody asked for.
